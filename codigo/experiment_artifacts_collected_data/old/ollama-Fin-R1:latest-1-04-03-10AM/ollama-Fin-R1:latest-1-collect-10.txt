3. **Function \(\alpha\)**: There exists a smooth function \(\alpha: D \to \mathbb{R}^+\) such that for all \(y_1, y_2 \in D\) (with \(y_1 \neq y_2\)):
   \[
   \int_D |\gamma(t) - \gamma(s)| dt \geq |y_1 - y_2| + 1.
   \]
4. **Supremum of \(x_1'\)**: \(\sup_{t \in D} x_1'(t) = M < +\infty\).
5. **Infimum of \(x_2'\)**: \(\inf_{s \in D} x_2'(s) > 0\).

### Step 2: Deriving Length Estimates
The condition on the integral provides a lower bound for the "average" distance between points on the curve over \(D\). Specifically, it implies that the total arc length of the curve restricted to \(D\) is large. Let's express this more formally.

#### Total Arc Length in \(D\)
Define the arc length functional:
\[
L_D = \int_D |\gamma'(t)| dt.
\]
Using the given inequality, for any distinct \(y_1, y_2 \in D\):
\[
\int_D |\gamma(t) - \gamma(s)| ds \geq |y_1 - y_2| + 1.
\]
Since this holds for all such \(y_1\) and \(y_2\), the average distance between points on \(D\) must be at least 1. Therefore:
\[
L_D \geq |y_1 - y_2| + 1 > 0.
\]

### Step 3: Relating Arc Length to Derivatives
Using the Mean Value Theorem for integrals, there exists some \(t^* \in D\) such that:
\[
L_D = \int_D |\gamma'(t)| dt \geq |D| \cdot \inf_{s \in D} |\gamma'(s)|,
\]
where \(|D|\) is the Lebesgue measure of \(D\). However, since we don't have information about \(\sup_{t \in D} |\gamma'(t)|\), this approach might not directly help. Instead, consider using the fact that the curve's derivatives are bounded.

### Step 4: Bounding the Curve’s Movement
From condition (4), \(\sup_{t \in D} x_1'(t) = M < +\infty\). This means \(x_1(t)\) has a maximum rate of change of \(M\) in \(D\). Similarly, from condition (5), there is a positive lower bound on the derivative of \(x_2(t)\).

#### Growth of \(x_1(t)\)
For any \(t \in D\):
\[
x_1(t) - x_1(t_0) = \int_{t_0}^t x_1'(s) ds \leq M |t - t_0|,
\]
implying that \(x_1(t)\) cannot change too rapidly. However, since the curve is in \(\mathbb{R}^2\), we need to consider both coordinates.

#### Growth of \(x_2(t)\)
Given \(\inf_{s \in D} x_2'(s) > 0\), there exists some \(\epsilon > 0\) such that:
\[
x_2(s) - x_2(s_0) \geq \epsilon |s - s_0| \quad \text{for all } s, s_0 \in D.
\]

### Step 5: Containment in an Open Ball
Assume for contradiction that the curve \(\gamma(t)\) is contained in an open ball \(B\) of radius \(r = \frac{2}{M^3}\). This implies:
\[
|\gamma(t) - \gamma(s)| < r = \frac{2}{M^3} \quad \text{for all } t, s \in [0, 1].
\]

### Step 6: Contradiction with Arc Length Estimate
From the arc length estimate derived earlier:
\[
L_D \geq |y_1 - y_2| + 1 > 1.
\]
However, if the curve is contained in a ball of radius \(r = \frac{2}{M^3}\), then the total variation in both coordinates must be limited. Specifically, since \(x_1\) and \(x_2\) are restricted to this ball:
\[
|x_1(t) - x_1(s)| < \frac{2}{M^3} \quad \text{and} \quad |x_2(t) - x_2(s)| < \frac{2}{M^3}.
\]
Thus, the total arc length \(L_D\) would be bounded by:
\[
L_D \leq 2 \left( \frac{2}{M^3} + \frac{2}{M^3} \right) = \frac{8}{M^3}.
\]
This contradicts the earlier estimate that \(L_D > 1\) (since \(|D|\), the measure of \(D\), is positive and finite).

### Step 7: Final Conclusion
The contradiction implies that our assumption—that the curve can be contained in a ball of radius \(\frac{2}{M^3}\)—must be false. Therefore, there must exist some point on the curve outside this ball. Hence, the curve cannot be fully contained within an open ball of radius \(\frac{2}{M^3}\).

### Final Answer
\[
\boxed{\text{The curve } \gamma(t) \text{ is not entirely contained within any open ball of radius } \frac{2}{M^3}.}
\]
